# The 15-Minute Agent
## AI Prompts & Workflows for Realtors

*Copy. Paste. Get your afternoon back.*

## Chapter 1 — The Problem Nobody Puts on the Business Card

You got into real estate to work with people. Then the day happens:

- the listing description you've rewritten four times,
- the lead from last night you haven't answered yet,
- the "just checking in" text you keep postponing,
- the closing timeline living half in your head and half in your inbox,
- the social post you know you should make.

None of that is selling. All of it is necessary.

**What the numbers say (read them carefully):**

- NAR's 2025 Technology Survey (1,241 responses): about **20% of agents use AI daily, 22% weekly**, and **32% haven't used it at all**. The most common use is generated content like listing descriptions (**46%**).
- Yet **46% said AI had no noticeable impact** on their business; 50% said moderately or significantly positive.
- Time-use figures floating around online (e.g., "10–15 hours a week on admin") mostly come from vendor blogs and small samples. Treat them as directional, not gospel. Track your own week; you'll learn more from that than from any statistic.

**The takeaway:** the agents getting value aren't using a secret tool. They're giving AI *context* and using *repeatable prompts*. That's all this guide is.

## Chapter 2 — Build Your "Agent Brain" (Soul File First, 30 Minutes)

Most agents get generic AI output because they give AI nothing to work with. The fix is two layers:

1. **Your SOUL file:** who you are, in your own words. This is what keeps AI from flattening your voice into "nestled, boasts, charming."
2. **Your Project instructions:** the practical facts (market, specialty, rules).

Put both inside a **Claude Project** and every workflow in Chapter 3 gets sharper, because you stop re-explaining yourself.

### Step 1: Build your SOUL file (about 10 minutes)

The free **Soulprint** tool from the Free Agent Movement ([buildsoulprint.freeagentmovement.io](https://buildsoulprint.freeagentmovement.io/)) runs a short, guided interview on your phone. You can answer out loud or typed, one question at a time, with no right answers. It then gives you:

| You get | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Your SOUL file** | A written identity document in your own words: your three words, your real "why," your "nevers," and the call only you can make |
| **Your Free Agent Score** | A read on where you stand across four shifts: **identity, systems, leverage, and visibility**, with one move to make on each. A mirror, not a grade |
| **A paste-anywhere prompt** | A clean version for ChatGPT, Claude, or any AI tool, so it speaks as you from the first message |

Download it as a PDF or copy the whole thing to your clipboard.

**What a good SOUL file sounds like.** Specific and a little uncomfortable. The tool's own sample, for a fictional agent, includes lines such as a personal meaning behind each of her three words, a "why" rooted in a family experience with a mortgage nobody explained, and "nevers" like *never push a couple into a number that breaks them in month four* and *never say "dream" when you mean "monthly payment."* Notice what makes it work: real stories, real limits, no marketing language. Yours should sound like you, not like anyone's sample.

**If you'd rather write it by hand,** answer these in plain, honest sentences:

```
1. Three words that describe how I work, and what each one really means to me.
2. Why I do this. The real reason, with a real moment behind it.
3. My nevers. Things I will not do, say, or push a client into,
   even if it costs me a deal.
4. The call only I can make. Where my judgment replaces any tool.
5. How I talk. Three real texts or emails I've sent that sound like me.
6. Words and phrases I'd never use.
```

### Step 2: Create the Claude Project

In Claude, create a Project called *Agent Brain*. Paste your **SOUL file** into the Project knowledge (or the top of the instructions), then add this below it:

```
You are my real estate assistant. I'm a licensed agent.
The SOUL file above is who I am. Match its voice, honor its nevers,
and never contradict it.

ABOUT MY BUSINESS
Name: [NAME] | Brokerage: [BROKERAGE] | Market: [CITIES/NEIGHBORHOODS]
Specialty: [first-time buyers / luxury / investors / relocation / etc.]
Typical client: [DESCRIBE]

HOW TO WORK
- Ask me for missing facts. Never invent property details, prices, or statistics.
- Draft only; I review before anything is sent.
- Never reference race, religion, familial status, disability, national origin,
  sex, or other protected characteristics when describing people or neighborhoods.
- If something needs legal or compliance review, say so.
```

### Step 3: Add your working notes

Add a short note with your active clients (first names and stage only), your active listings, and your typical transaction timeline.

> **Privacy rule of thumb:** use first names and stages, not SSNs, financial account details, or full contract PDFs unless your brokerage has approved the tool for that data.

### Step 4: Use your Free Agent Score as your roadmap

Your score points to one move on each of the four shifts. Map them to this guide:

| Shift | Meaning | Start with |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity** | Clear on who you are and who you serve | Your SOUL file (this chapter) |
| **Systems** | Repeatable processes instead of memory | Workflows 1, 3, 5 |
| **Leverage** | Your time multiplied by tools and help | Workflows 2, 6 |
| **Visibility** | People know you and think of you | Workflows 4, 7 |

Pick the shift with your lowest score and start with those workflows.

## Chapter 3 — Seven Workflows

### Workflow 1: The Morning Briefing (5 minutes)

**When:** Before you open email.

```
Good morning. Using what you know about my business and the notes below,
give me, on one page:
1. My top 3 priorities today
2. Clients I haven't contacted in 7+ days
3. One sphere outreach to send today: who + a draft text
4. A 3-sentence market talking point for conversations or social

Notes: [Paste today's calendar, open tasks, and who you last spoke to.]
```

**Make it better:** end each day by pasting tomorrow's calendar and unfinished tasks. Your briefing writes itself.

---

### Workflow 2: Listing Launch (10 minutes instead of 90)

```
New listing: [ADDRESS, beds/baths/sqft, price, 3–5 real features,
one personal detail about the home or owners].

Create:
1. A 150-word MLS description. Facts only, no filler.
2. Three Instagram captions: lifestyle, data-driven, story.
3. An email to my sphere asking for referrals.
4. A 2-sentence text for buyer's agents.
5. A checklist of facts you assumed or couldn't verify.

Match my voice (see my SOUL file). Fair Housing safe: describe the property, not the people.
```

**Worked example (output style):**
> *"Three bedrooms, two baths, 1,850 sq ft. New roof (2023), kitchen opens to a fenced backyard with mature pecan trees. Walk to Cole Park..."*

**Always verify:** square footage, school names, HOA terms, and anything you didn't personally confirm. Item 5 exists to catch AI guesses. **Never** let a listing go live without checking it against your MLS data.

---

### Workflow 3: Speed-to-Lead Follow-Up (3 minutes)

The first agent to respond usually has the advantage. AI helps you respond *well*, fast.

```
A new lead came in. Here is what I know: [name, source, what they asked,
price range, timeline].

Write:
1. A text under 40 words that answers their actual question
   and asks ONE easy question.
2. An email version with a clear next step (call or showing).
3. A follow-up to send if they don't reply in 24 hours.
4. A follow-up for day 4 that gives value (not "just checking in").
```

**Tip:** save your best-performing replies into the Agent Brain so it learns what works for *your* market.

---

### Workflow 4: Sphere (SOI) Check-Ins

```
I need to text [FIRST NAME]. What I know: [last time we talked,
something personal, where they are in their housing journey].

Write a text that:
- sounds like me, not a mass blast
- mentions something real about them
- is under 3 sentences
- makes no sales ask
- ends with something that's easy to reply to
Give me 3 variations.
```

**Make it better:** keep a running notes doc of personal details (kids' sports, pets, new jobs). Paste the relevant lines in. Specificity is the entire trick.

---

### Workflow 5: Transaction Deadline Tracker

```
Here are the key dates and terms from my contract (typed in, not the PDF):
[effective date, option period, financing deadline, inspection, appraisal,
closing date, other contingencies].

Create:
1. A dated timeline of every deadline
2. A weekly checklist for the next 14 days
3. A client-friendly email explaining "what happens next"
4. Questions I should confirm with my broker or TC
```

**Guardrail:** AI can mis-calculate dates and doesn't know your state's contract nuances. Verify every date against the actual contract.

---

### Workflow 6: Client Q&A Replies

```
A client asked: "[PASTE QUESTION]"
Context: [stage, relevant facts].

Write a reply that is clear, calm, and under 120 words.
Explain the concept simply. Tell me if this needs a lawyer,
lender, or broker input before I send it.
```

Great for: "What's an appraisal gap?", "Why did the inspector flag this?", "Should we waive a contingency?" (here, AI drafts the explanation; **you** give the advice.)

---

### Workflow 7: The Weekly Market Post

```
Write a 120-word market update for [AREA]. Use ONLY these numbers:
[median price, days on market, inventory, months of supply — from your MLS].
Tone: plain English, no predictions. End with one question that invites a reply.
Also give me a 20-second video script version.
```

**Rule:** AI has no live MLS access. Feed it your own data, cite the source in the post.

## Chapter 4 — Stop Renting AI. Build Four Assistants

Chapter 3 gave you single-shot prompts. Those are fast, but every chat starts from zero. Agents who get the most from AI do something different: they **build an assistant once**, and it already knows their business every time they use it. (This "renting vs. owning" idea comes from Brian Force's free [Real Estate Agent's Guide to Claude](https://getresidualagent.com/claude-guide); the prompts below are written fresh for this guide.)

### The shortcut: have Claude write your prompts

You don't need to be good at prompting. A good prompt has four parts: **Role** (who Claude should be), **Context** (the background), **Task** (the job), and **Format** (how you want the answer). Or let Claude handle it. Paste this once and it becomes your prompt coach:

```
Act as my prompt coach. I'm a real estate agent, and I'm not always sure
how to ask for what I want. When I describe a task, first ask me up to two
short questions if something important is missing. If my request is
low-value, suggest a sharper version of what I'm really trying to achieve.
Then write a finished, ready-to-use prompt in my voice, in its own
copy block. Underneath, label the Role, Context, Task, and Format you used
in one line each, so I learn the pattern.
```

### The pattern for every assistant

> **1.** Paste a *builder* prompt. **2.** Answer Claude's questions, one at a time. **3.** Paste the instructions it writes into a new Claude Project. **4.** Upload the files it asks for. **5.** Use the *run* prompt whenever you need it.

Your SOUL file (Chapter 2) goes into each Project too, so every assistant sounds like you. Start with whichever pain is biggest. For most agents that's transactions.

---

### Assistant 1: The Transaction Assistant

**What it does:** you give it an executed contract and it handles intake: key dates, intro emails to every party, and a critical-date timeline.

**Builder prompt**
```
You're an expert at setting up Claude for real estate agents. Help me write
custom instructions for a Claude Project that will be my transaction
coordination assistant. When I give it a new executed contract, it should
do intake the way I would.
Interview me ONE question at a time, building on each answer. Cover:
my transaction process, the critical dates I track (option period, financing,
appraisal, closing), the intro emails I send and to whom (client, lender,
title, co-op agent), my preferred vendors, what I want flagged or
double-checked on every contract, and my voice.
When you have enough, give me three things: (1) finished custom instructions
to paste into the Project, (2) a short list of files to upload as knowledge,
and (3) the message I'll send for each new deal.
Ask your first question now.
```

**Files to upload when asked:** your transaction timeline/checklist, email templates, preferred vendor list.

**Run prompt**
```
Here's a new executed contract: [paste or attach]. Run intake: list the key
dates, draft the intro emails, and build the critical-date timeline.
Flag anything unusual or missing.
```

> **Guardrail:** AI can misread contracts and miscalculate dates. Verify every date and term against the signed contract and with your broker or transaction coordinator.

---

### Assistant 2: The Content Machine

**What it does:** feed it your monthly market data and it produces a full set of client-ready content in your voice. Never stare at a blank page again.

**Builder prompt**
```
You're an expert at setting up Claude for real estate agents. Help me write
custom instructions for a Claude Project that will be my ongoing content
machine: it turns my market data into client-ready content in my voice
every month.
Interview me ONE question at a time. Cover: my market and niche, who the
content is for, my brand voice (ask me to paste a writing sample), exactly
what I want produced each month and in what format, where my market data
comes from, and how I like to review drafts.
When you have enough, give me: (1) finished custom instructions,
(2) a list of files to upload, and (3) the message I'll send each month.
Ask your first question now.
```

**Files to upload:** a few of your best past posts, your market-specific data sources, your brand voice notes.

**Run prompt**
```
Here's this month's market data: [paste or attach]. Produce my full content
set in my voice, ready to publish. Cite the data source in each piece.
```

> **Guardrail:** AI has no live MLS access. It only knows the numbers you give it. Check every figure before posting.

---

### Assistant 3: The Referral & Database Engine

**What it does:** your past clients and sphere are the business you already own. This assistant turns them into repeat business and referrals without sounding salesy.

**Builder prompt**
```
You're an expert at setting up Claude for real estate agents. Help me write
custom instructions for a Claude Project that will be my referral and
database engine: it turns my past clients and sphere into repeat business
and referrals without feeling salesy.
Interview me ONE question at a time. Cover: how big my database is and how
it's split (past clients, sphere, dormant leads, vendors), what data I
actually have on each contact (last transaction, home anniversary, life
details, lead source) and where it lives, my voice and how I like to reach
out (text, email, call, handwritten), what a "win" looks like (referrals,
repeat deals, reviews, reactivated leads), and how much time I can
realistically give this each week.
When you have enough, give me: (1) finished custom instructions,
(2) a list of files to upload, and (3) the first message to kick it off.
Ask your first question now.
```

**Run prompt**
```
From my contact list, pick five past clients I haven't spoken to in over a
year who are worth reconnecting with now. Write each a short, warm message
that uses something specific you know about them (purchase anniversary,
neighborhood, or a life detail) so it feels personal, not mass-sent.
Reconnect, don't pitch. Under 50 words each, in my voice. Tell me why you
picked each person, and ask if you're missing a detail.
```

> **Guardrail:** Follow your state and federal rules on texting and email consent (for example, TCPA and CAN-SPAM) and respect do-not-contact requests. Keep contact data limited to what you need.

### Assistant 4: The Listing Presentation Engine

**What it does:** build your master listing presentation once. For every appointment, Claude customizes it for that property and those sellers, so you walk in tailored instead of generic.

**Builder prompt**
```
You're an expert at setting up Claude for real estate agents. Help me build
a Claude Project that will be my listing presentation engine: it holds my
master listing presentation, and for each new appointment it customizes
that template for the specific property and sellers.
Interview me ONE question at a time. Cover: what makes me different and why
a seller should choose me (any stats, track record, or testimonials I can
point to), the markets and price points I work in, the sellers and
objections I face most, the sections I want (about me, pricing strategy,
marketing plan, comps, commission and value, timeline, next steps), how I
present it (slides, PDF leave-behind, in person), and my voice.
When you have enough, give me: (1) finished custom instructions,
(2) a polished master presentation template to save as Project knowledge,
and (3) the message I'll send for each new listing.
Ask your first question now.
```

**Files to upload:** your current presentation (if any), your stats and track record, real testimonials.

**Run prompt**
```
I have a new listing appointment.
Property: [address, type, condition, target price].
Sellers: [names, why selling, timeline, concerns].
Market notes/comps: [paste or attach].
Customize my master presentation for this property and these sellers:
tailor the pricing strategy and marketing plan to this home, speak to what
these sellers care about, pre-empt their likely objections, and flag the
single point I most need to land. Keep it in my voice and ready to present.
```

**Bonus: rehearse before you walk in**
```
Role-play with me. You're a skeptical seller who thinks my commission is
too high and wants to try selling it themselves first. Push back hard.
After each of my answers, tell me what landed and give me a stronger version.
```

> **Guardrail:** only use real stats and real testimonials. Never let AI invent either. Comps must come from your MLS.

### Next level: make it run on its own (Claude Cowork)

A Project waits until you ask. **Claude Cowork** is the agent version: it can work with your files, connect to tools like Gmail and Drive, and run recurring tasks on a schedule. Features and availability change, so check Anthropic's current plan and platform details before relying on it. Have Claude design the automation:

```
I want to turn this Project into a recurring task in Claude Cowork so it
runs on its own. Act as my automation consultant. Ask me what should
happen, on what schedule, where the data comes from, and where the output
should go. Then write the exact recurring-task instruction to set up in
Cowork. Keep it to one clear, repeatable task.
```

Start with the Projects. Reach for Cowork when you're ready to automate.

## Chapter 5 — Guardrails (The Part That Protects Your License)

1. **Fair Housing.** Describe properties, not people. Avoid phrases like "perfect for young families" or "safe neighborhood." Ask AI to review drafts: *"Flag any language that could violate Fair Housing."* Still check it yourself.
2. **Verify facts.** Prices, dates, square footage, taxes, school info, flood zones, and legal claims all get checked at the source.
3. **Privacy.** Don't paste sensitive client data into tools your brokerage hasn't approved.
4. **Brokerage/MLS rules.** Some require approval or disclosure for AI-generated content, and virtual staging has specific MLS disclosure rules in many areas. Ask your broker.
5. **No legal or financial advice.** AI drafts the explanation; refer the question to the right professional.
6. **Be honest.** Never fabricate testimonials, reviews, or stats.

## Chapter 6 — Your 30-Day Plan

| Week | Do this | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Build Agent Brain; run Morning Briefing daily | 20 min + 5/day |
| 2 | Build ONE assistant from Chapter 4 (transactions for most agents); use Listing Launch and Speed-to-Lead replies | 30 min + 15/day |
| 3 | Run your assistant on a real deal; add 5 SOI texts per day | 15 min/day |
| 4 | Build the next assistant (Content Machine or Referral Engine); review what worked and update your Brain | 45 min |

**Measure it:** write down hours of admin this week vs. week 4. If it didn't save time, change the prompts, not your expectations.
